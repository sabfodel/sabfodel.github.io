Three months in. Writing this down mostly so that in a year I can look back and see whether
any of it was true.

## What's improved

**Bow hold.** For the first month my hand was a claw and my tone was scratch. The change came
from stopping thinking about grip and starting thinking about weight — the arm is heavy, the
bow just transmits it. Once that clicked the sound opened up considerably.

**Left hand frame.** First position is roughly where it should be now, without hunting. Not
reliably, but the shape exists.

## What hasn't

**Intonation.** This is the whole problem. No frets means every note is a decision, and a
decision that's off by a couple of millimetres is audibly wrong. The unforgiving thing is
that you hear it perfectly well and still can't correct it fast enough.

What helps: playing very slowly against a drone. What doesn't help: playing at speed and
hoping. Discovered that one the hard way.

**Vibrato.** Barely started. Currently produces something closer to a tremor.

## Odd overlap with physics

The bit that surprised me is how much practising resembles working through a derivation.
When something is wrong you can't fix "the whole thing" — you have to isolate the step. Two
bars, hands separate, slow. It's the same instinct as stripping a problem down to the one
term that's misbehaving.

Also: both punish you for pretending you understand something you don't.

## Next three months

- Scales in three octaves, properly, not sloppily
- Actually start vibrato rather than continuing to avoid it
- One piece taken all the way to finished, instead of five half-learned
