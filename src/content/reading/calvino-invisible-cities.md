Not a novel so much as a structure. Marco Polo describes city after impossible city to an
ageing Kublai Khan, and slowly you work out that he's describing the same city every time,
or perhaps describing the act of describing.

What got me is how *architectural* it is. The cities are arranged in a pattern — a formal
scheme that Calvino never announces but that you start to feel around the halfway point. It
reminded me, uncomfortably, of reading a good proof: the pleasure is in noticing the shape
underneath.

**The line I keep returning to:** the one about how the city's answers are less interesting
than the questions it's asked. I've thought about that one in contexts it was never meant
for.

**Read it slowly.** Two or three cities an evening. Reading it straight through flattens the
whole thing into a list, which is precisely what it isn't.
