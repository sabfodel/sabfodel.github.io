I probably wouldn't have fallen for quantum mechanics without this book. Griffiths writes as
if he can hear the objection forming in your head and answers it in a footnote — very few
textbooks manage that.

**Best chapter:** perturbation theory and fine structure. Building up where the small
splittings in the hydrogen spectrum come from, one correction at a time, is genuinely
satisfying.

**Complaint:** he moves through the measurement problem far too quickly. It's tucked into an
afterword, which is odd given it's the part that actually keeps people up at night.

**Afterwards:** I reread chapter 11 several times and eventually wrote it up properly —
see [[berry-phase]].
